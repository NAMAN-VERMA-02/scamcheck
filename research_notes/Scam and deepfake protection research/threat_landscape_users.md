# Global Scam & Deepfake Threat Landscape and Victim User Journeys (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 24 Oct 2026. Most "latest" official data covers calendar year 2025, published Feb to Oct 2026. Prior-year data is labelled. Source-quality tags: [OFFICIAL] = regulator or law enforcement; [INDUSTRY] = bank or industry body; [ACADEMIC]; [VENDOR/SURVEY] = vendor marketing or self-reported polling, weaker evidence.

## Q1: Regional picture (top scam types by loss and count, channels, payment rails, protection infrastructure)

### Takeaway
In every region with good data, **investment scams are the biggest source of losses**. They are usually crypto-settled and recruited via social media or messaging apps: US $8.6bn, UK 38% of APP losses, India 77% of losses, Australia $838m, Singapore #1. **Impersonation scams lead by count and are growing fastest** in several markets: government or official impersonation and "digital arrest" in India and Singapore, and imposter scams in the US. Protection infrastructure differs widely:
- **UK:** mandatory reimbursement.
- **Singapore:** shared responsibility, limited to phishing.
- **Australia:** obligations on platforms, telcos and banks, with codes not yet in force.
- **India:** rapid-freeze helpline.
- **US:** essentially none for authorised payments.

### Inferences
- **Investment fraud is the single largest harm everywhere**, typically crypto-settled and social-media/messaging originated. In the US, crypto accounts for more than half of all IC3 losses ($11.4bn of $20.9bn).
- **Fastest growing:** government-official / "digital arrest" impersonation (Singapore up 124% in cases; a known India hotspot), UK investment APP (up 40%), US elder losses (up 59%) and crypto ATM losses (up 58%).
- Reimbursement regimes (UK) reduce net losses to victims but appear to displace fraud rather than stop it. **No regime covers authorised investment scams settled in crypto.** This is the largest coverage gap.

### Gaps
- No primary data surfaced on channel shares for India (WhatsApp, Telegram, calls), LatAm outside Brazil (Mexico, Argentina, Colombia), continental EU (Europol IOCTA 2025/2026 was not searched) or Africa (no quantified losses).
- No authoritative per-channel split of WhatsApp vs Telegram vs SMS for most regions. The US social-media figure is the main hard data point.
- **I found no reliable data on regional language or code-switching (Hinglish, Taglish, Spanglish) as a scam factor.** The searches returned nothing usable.
- Brazil MED 2.0 status could not be confirmed. US Zelle/CFPB reimbursement status for 2026 was not checked.

## Q2: Which categories cause the greatest harm

### Takeaway
By dollars, the ranking is **investment/pig-butchering > BEC/invoice redirection > tech-support / government / bank impersonation > romance**. These are high-value, low-count categories. Phishing/smishing, extortion, marketplace (non-delivery) and job scams are high-count but lower-value per case. Job/task scams and government impersonation are the high-growth categories in Asia.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] US loss ranking: Investment $8.65bn > BEC $3.05bn > Tech support $2.13bn > Romance $929m > Government impersonation $798m. Phishing is #1 by count (191,561) but only $216m in losses — [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] UK: investment is the largest APP category (38% of losses). Unauthorised fraud (card, account takeover) is still a bigger total (£703m) — [UK Finance 2026](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/news-and-insight/press-release/fraud-report-2026-press-release)
- [OFFICIAL] Singapore top five by loss: investment, government impersonation, job, phishing, BEC — [SPF brief](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Advisories/Scams/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] Australia: investment, then payment redirection (business invoice fraud), then romance — [NASC 2025](https://www.nasc.gov.au/system/files/targeting-scams-report-2025.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] India: investment about 77% and digital arrest about 8% of losses — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fraud-alert-india-is-losing-over-22000-crore-a-year-in-cyber-scams-and-the-worst-is-yet-to-come/80612.html)
- [OFFICIAL] FTC: imposter scams are the most common by count (about 1/3 of reports) and investment scams are the largest by loss ($7.9bn) — [FTC Jun 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025)

### Inferences
- **Underserved and hard to detect:** investment and romance scams. They are long-con, the victim authorises the payments and believes them, payments are spread over weeks, and money often leaves via the victim's own crypto exchange account. Bank transaction monitoring sees "legitimate" self-transfers.
- **Easier to detect:** impersonation calls (scripted, urgent, recognisable patterns) and phishing links.
- **SMEs:** BEC and payment redirection remain the #2 harm by value and are largely a wire/ACH problem.

### Gaps
- No standardised cross-country taxonomy exists. "Job/task scam" appears as its own category in Singapore and India (embedded in investment), while IC3 "Employment" is only $363m.

## Q3: Voice-clone / deepfake prevalence: hard evidence vs hype

### Takeaway
The hard evidence shows **AI is a real but still minor, explicitly identified share of losses**. IC3 2025 recorded $893M across 22,364 AI-referenced complaints, about 4% of total losses, and only **just over $5M in voice-clone "distress" scams**. The headline vendor statistics ("$1.8bn voice-clone losses", "3,892% surge") lack primary sources. AI is most material as a scaling tool for investment and romance scripts, not as cinematic deepfakes. That said, under-attribution is likely.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] IC3 2025 AI descriptor: **22,364 complaints, $893.3M adjusted losses**.
  - AI-nexus investment complaints exceeded $632M.
  - BEC involving AI exceeded $30M.
  - Romance/confidence with a likely AI nexus exceeded $19M.
  - **Distress / grandparent scams using cloned voices exceeded $5M.**
  - The FBI treats the AI descriptor as overlapping with crime categories; it is a floor, not a deepfake-only count — [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [summary](https://www.neuraldefend.com/insights/topics/deepfake-statistics)
- [OFFICIAL] Users aged 60+: 3,143 AI-related complaints, more than $352M — [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/)
- [OFFICIAL] IC3 also flags voice and video deepfakes in remote job interviews, where lip movement and audio do not match — [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC] Raza (Vector Institute), "The Deepfakes We Missed" (arXiv 2605.12075, May 2026), argues:
  - The predicted political / face-swap video threat largely did not materialise. There was "no documented case" of a deepfake decisively altering a 2024 election.
  - Real harms are peer-generated NCII, voice-clone scam calls and emotional-manipulation fraud.
  - Voice-clone fraud is likely **absorbed into BEC and other statistics** (undercounted).
  - It cites Arup Hong Kong (early 2024, US$25M, a multi-person deepfake video call) as the most prominent verified enterprise case — [arXiv 2605.12075](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12075v1)
- [ACADEMIC] Humans cannot reliably detect speech deepfakes (Mai et al., 2023) — [arXiv 2301.07829](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07829)
- [VENDOR/SURVEY] McAfee 2026: 1 in 10 Americans report having experienced a voice-clone scam, and 1 in 4 experienced one or know someone who has. These are self-reported and the two figures are internally inconsistent — [afterlife.ai aggregator](https://www.afterlife.ai/ai-voice-cloning-statistics); [digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/deepfake-statistics-2026-fraud-detection-data)
- [Fact-check] The widely repeated "$1.8bn lost to AI voice-cloning scams in 2025" figure has **no primary source**. Most attack-surface data comes from vendors selling detection — [netarx / aggregator commentary](https://www.netarx.com/blog/deepfake-statistics-2026); [Memeburn "3,892% surge"](https://memeburn.com/deepfake-statistics-2026-reveal-a-3892-fraud-surge/) is an example of an unsourced headline.

### Inferences
- In consumer contexts, voice-clone family-emergency scams are real but small in verified dollars ($5M in the US). Survey "exposure" rates of 10-25% are probably inflated by mislabelling ordinary "hi mum" text or impersonation calls as voice cloning.
- The bigger AI effect is invisible: LLM-generated scripts and translation, which let compounds run multilingual pig-butchering at scale. Most of the $632M AI-nexus investment losses fall here.
- Enterprise deepfake executive calls are rare but very high-value per incident (Arup at $25M). Their rarity argues for process controls (call-back verification) over media detection.

### Gaps
- No law-enforcement voice-clone figures were found for the UK, India, Singapore or Australia. The academic study with 1,768 participants judging TTS deepfakes (2026) was not retrieved in full.

## Q4: User segments

### Takeaway
- **Older adults (60+)** lose far more per incident and in total. US: $7.75bn, about 37% of IC3 losses. Their exposure is to investment, tech-support and government impersonation, often paid via crypto ATMs, wire or cash.
- **Young adults** report losses more often but smaller amounts (job, marketplace, investment, social media).
- **Small businesses** face BEC and invoice redirection, the #2 category by value.
- **Families** face distress/voice-clone scams that are high-emotion but low in verified volume.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] US 60+: 201,266 complaints and **$7.75bn (up 59%)**. Investment $3.52bn; crypto-linked $4.35bn. **FTC: 60+ losses rose fivefold, from $600M (2020) to $3bn (2025)** (report to Congress, 19 Oct 2026) — [IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [PYMNTS on FTC](https://pymnts.com/cybersecurity/fraud-prevention/2026/ftc-data-shows-investment-scams-fuel-fivefold-surge-in-seniors-fraud-losses)
- [OFFICIAL] FTC: older adults report losing money **less often** than younger adults but have much higher median losses, at **$2,000 for 80+**. A small number of losses above $100k make up most of older adults' dollars — [FTC older consumers (via search)](https://www.ftc.gov/node/338049)
- [Secondary of FTC 2024 data, prior-year] 20-29s: about 40-44% of those reporting fraud lost money, with a median of about $400-417. For 70+, about 18% lost money; 80+ median about $1,092. Figures vary by source — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/financial-protection/scams-targeting-you-most-age-group); [CUNA](https://www.cunastrategicservices.com/content/who-gets-scammed-the-most)
- [OFFICIAL] Singapore: under-50s were more than 60% of victims in H1 2025; 21% of 65+ victims fell to government impersonation — [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/08/youths-adults-scam-victims-spf/); [SPF](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Advisories/Scams/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] Australia: 65+ are 26.5% of Scamwatch losses vs 17.1% of the population — [NASC](https://www.nasc.gov.au/system/files/targeting-scams-report-2025.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] Businesses: BEC $3.05bn in the US. Australian payment redirection A$166.8M. BEC is about 86% wire/ACH — [IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [NASC](https://www.nasc.gov.au/system/files/targeting-scams-report-2025.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC] FINRA Foundation (Feb 2025, 905 known US fraud victims): **loneliness, financial or emotional precarity, older age and risky financial behaviour** are associated with more victimisation — [FINRA Foundation brief](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2025-02/research-brief-older-adults-fraud-risk.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC] Chanenson et al. (Google, CHI '26) found that **financial insecurity and legal precarity** (e.g., job seekers, migrants) raise engagement with scams. The title quotes a victim: "It didn't feel right but I needed a job so desperately" — [arXiv 2604.06218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06218)
- [ACADEMIC] Less affluent countries show more financial loss and less knowledge of how to report — [Houtti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12896)

### Inferences
- For **elderly users**, value lies in high-value transaction interruption and trusted-contact loops: crypto ATM, wire and large transfers.
- For **young users**, value lies in pre-payment scam checks for job offers, marketplace listings and investment "mentors" on social media and Telegram.
- For **SMEs**, value lies in out-of-band payee-change verification.
- For **families**, the answer is a pre-agreed verification ("safe word") protocol, which is cheap and robust against voice cloning.

### Gaps
- No hard data found for regional-language or code-switching users (Hinglish, Taglish, Spanglish). The Houtti et al. GNI finding is the closest proxy.
- No dedicated data on parents receiving fake distress calls beyond IC3's $5M.

## Q5: Victim journey: before, during, before payment, after loss

### Takeaway
Scams compress decisions: **43% of US victims paid within one hour of first contact**. They exploit fear (impersonation) or hope (investment, job), and they isolate victims from the people who might stop them. After loss, recovery is rare (4% to 34%, depending on source and definition). Reporting is low because of shame (about 7-32% report, depending on source). Victims often feel doubt ("it didn't feel right") but proceed anyway because of need or pressure. The failure is in acting on suspicion, not only in detecting it.

### Cited Findings
- [SURVEY, PYMNTS Jul 2026, n=9,524 US] **43% of victims paid within one hour of first contact; 18% took two weeks or more** — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/report/study/frauds-loyalty-tax-how-scams-cost-banks-their-customers/)
- [ACADEMIC] Scammers exploit fear and hope. Help needs differ by stage (before, during, after). Precarity drives people to continue despite doubts — [Chanenson et al., CHI '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06218)
- [SURVEY, non-vendor] 66% of US victims recovered nothing (2025) — [Stop Scams Alliance/Gallup via CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/23/americans-lost-an-estimated-68-billion-to-scams-in-2025/187222/)
- [VENDOR/SURVEY] GASA: only 4% of victims fully recover (2024/2025). Reporting figures conflict: 59% never report vs 74% report — [Feedzai](https://www.feedzai.com/blog/gasa-global-state-of-scams-report/); [GASA](https://gasa.org/)
- [SURVEY] UK: 32% reported to authorities; 42% did not tell their bank; two-thirds told no friend or family member. Victims felt angry with themselves (46%), stupid (40%) and embarrassed (38%) — [NTS 2023](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/news/19-million-lose-money-to-scams-but-fewer-than-a-third-report/)
- [VENDOR/SURVEY] F-Secure 2025: only 7% of scams are reported globally. **69% believe they can spot scams, yet 43% of those were victimised in the past year** (overconfidence) — [F-Secure report](https://www.f-secure.com/i/x/6a979e8a7d/2025-09-24_scam-report.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC] A systematic review (Computers in Human Behavior, 2025) finds shame and embarrassment often outweigh the desire for justice among older victims. Scams are under-acknowledged even in surveys (DeLiema, Shadel & Pak 2019) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563225001815)
- [ACADEMIC] Qualitative study (2025): victim-survivors and their support networks describe "falling into a black hole". Victims underplay impacts because of stigma — [Taylor & Francis, Victims & Offenders](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15564886.2025.2481267)
- [ACADEMIC] Factors in reporting fraud to police (2025) — [Psychology, Crime & Law](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1068316X.2025.2468347)

### Inferences
- **Before contact:** risk is driven by precarity (job seekers, lonely or isolated elders) and by social-media exposure. Social media is the costliest US contact method.
- **During:** urgency (impersonation), isolation (the instruction "don't tell your bank or family" is characteristic of digital arrest), and moving the conversation to WhatsApp or Telegram. The channel-switch claim is widely reported but not quantified in the sources above.
- **Just before payment:** the decision window is short (under 1 hour for 43%). Doubt is often present. Victims are often coached to lie to bank staff.
- **After loss:** shame suppresses reporting and slows freezes, and speed of reporting is what determines recovery (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No primary study quantifying the "move to another app" step or how often victims are coached to lie to banks.
- Found no peer-reviewed field experiment directly testing *why people don't verify*. Overconfidence (F-Secure) and precarity (Chanenson) are the closest evidence.

## Q6: Highest-value intervention points (and what demonstrably works)

### Takeaway
The strongest *measured* evidence is for two things:
1. **Fast post-loss freezing.** IC3 RAT froze 58% of attempted funds; India's CFCFRMS has saved more than ₹11,158 crore.
2. **Liability-shifting regimes** that push banks to add friction. UK Faster Payments APP losses fell about 21%; Frontier estimates about £73M per year fewer losses.

Banks rate **pausing or blocking transactions** as the most effective control. Generic warnings and awareness campaigns decay quickly. Confirmation of Payee does not stop scams where the victim pays a mule. Rigorous RCT evidence on warnings, cooling-off periods and "call a trusted person" was not found.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] IC3 Recovery Asset Team / Financial Fraud Kill Chain 2025: **3,900 incidents, $1.164bn attempted theft, $679M frozen (58% success)**.
  - Domestic: 3,574 incidents, $507M frozen. International: 326 incidents, $172M frozen.
  - 60+: 642 incidents, $32.9M of $65.4M frozen.
  - The kill chain only works if the complaint is filed quickly. ATO cases can fan out to 50+ recipient accounts — [IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] India CFCFRMS/1930: more than ₹11,158 crore saved across more than 32.80 lakh complaints (cumulative to Jun 2026), against about ₹19,800-22,500 crore lost in 2025 alone — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287039&reg=48&lang=2)
- [OFFICIAL/CONSULTANCY] UK mandatory reimbursement: about 21% fall in Faster Payments APP losses (PSR). Frontier Economics estimates about £73M per year fewer. Overall APP losses still rose 19% in 2025 — [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/7e5065fefef1466cadd1b2b27185fab2-5HjdcNX_2/); [UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/news-and-insight/press-release/fraud-report-2026-press-release)
- [OFFICIAL] Singapore cases fell 27.6% and losses fell 17.9% in 2025, the first-ever decline. This followed SRF, Money Lock, the ScamShield ecosystem and the Protection from Scams Act measures. Correlational only — [SPF](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/police-life/2026/02/scams-and-cybercrime-fell-by-almost-a-quarter-in-2025)
- [INDUSTRY SURVEY] KPMG Global Banking Scam Survey (Feb 2025):
  - Banks rate **pausing or blocking transactions as the most effective** prevention.
  - Awareness campaigns are "only effective for a short time".
  - Confirmation of Payee is "less effective" against scams but an expected minimum.
  - Customer frustration with friction is a top complaint — [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2025/kpmg-global-banking-scam-survey-2025.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Generic "is this a scam?" banners get clicked through. Warnings tied to the stated payment purpose and live risk signals, plus a human conversation for the highest risk, are claimed to work better. This is unverified — [NHIMG guide](https://nhimg.org/authorised-push-payment-app-fraud-guide); [Experian blog citing an unnamed study on risk-based + call-to-action warnings](https://www.experian.com/blogs/insights/authorized-push-payment-fraud/)
- [OFFICIAL/INDUSTRY] Confirmation of Payee / Verification of Payee catches misdirected payments but not scams where the victim pays a mule or their own crypto account — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/protecting-consumers-from-financial-scams-and-frauds_d41817bb-en/full-report/component-7.html); [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2025/kpmg-global-banking-scam-survey-2025.pdf)
- [ACADEMIC] Braithwaite (LSE, 2024) offers a legal analysis of APP fraud liability — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/123798/4/Braithwaite_authorised-push-payment--published.pdf); "The view from below: Resistance and change in APP fraud" (2025) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791425000429). Not read in full.

### Inferences
Ranked by value, inferred from the evidence above:
1. **Transaction interruption at the moment of first high-risk payment.** 43% of victims pay within an hour, so a short delay plus a contextual check catches a large share. Most valuable for crypto on-ramps, crypto ATMs and first payments to new payees.
2. **Reporting assistance and rapid freeze.** It has the strongest measured payoff (58% frozen when fast). Shame and not knowing how to report are the bottleneck, so frictionless, non-judgmental reporting has real value.
3. **Pre-payment scam-check / detection** of messages, calls, job offers and investment platforms. It is useful for high-count categories (phishing, impersonation, job). It is weak for long-con investment and romance scams, where trust is already established.
4. **Identity verification.** Out-of-band call-back and family safe words are very high-value for BEC and executive deepfakes, and for family distress calls. Deepfake *media detection* has low marginal value given the small verified volumes and poor human or automated reliability.
5. **Recovery after funds leave.** Low value: recovery is about 4% to 34%, and crypto dissipates quickly.
- "Call a trusted person" interventions fit the evidence that victims are isolated: two-thirds tell no one, and victims are instructed not to. However, **no outcome data was found**.

### Gaps
- **No randomised or field-experiment evidence was found** on the effect sizes of bank warnings, cooling-off delays, trusted-contact nudges or bank call-backs. Specific searches (Starling, Monzo, UPI first-transfer delays, warning-fatigue studies) returned no primary studies.
- Recommended next searches: FCA / Behavioural Insights Team evaluations; Lloyds or TSB published warning trials; RBI and NPCI UPI controls; MAS evaluations of Money Lock and cooling-off periods.
- Singapore Money Lock 2026 uptake data was not found.
