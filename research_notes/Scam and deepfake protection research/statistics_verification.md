# Verification of Global Scam and Fraud Loss Statistics (as of 24 Oct 2026)

Method note: research ran 24 Oct 2026. ftc.gov refused direct connections (ECONNREFUSED) and the McAfee PDF timed out. FTC figures are therefore confirmed through FTC-hosted URLs that show up in search results, plus close secondary coverage. Confidence is lowered where that applies. "Reported" means losses that victims reported to an agency. "Estimated" means a modelled figure adjusted for underreporting. "Survey" means respondents' own recall, sometimes extrapolated to a population.

## Claim 1: GASA Global State of Scams 2025 ($442B, 42 countries, ~46,000 respondents, 23% lost money)

### Takeaway
The report is real. The figure is $442B lost across 42 countries, from a survey of 46,000 adults, co-published with Feedzai around Oct 2025. It is a survey-based extrapolation, not reported losses, and should be labelled "estimated (survey-extrapolated)". The "23% reported losses" part is UNVERIFIED as worded. The primary page I could read uses "23%" only in an unclear phrase, and secondary sources give conflicting prevalence figures (57% "scammed", 70% "encountered"). Confidence: high for $442B / 42 countries / 46,000; low for 23%.

### Cited Findings
- Feedzai, co-presenter of GASA's report: "consumers from 42 countries lost roughly $442 billion to scams in the past year". It also says "GASA's report surveyed 46,000 adults worldwide". The blog is dated 9 Oct 2025 and gives no calculation method or fieldwork vendor. — [Feedzai blog](https://www.feedzai.com/blog/gasa-global-state-of-scams-report/)
- The same Feedzai page says "57% of respondents were scammed in the past year". Elsewhere it uses "encountered" for apparently the same figure. The only 23% on the page is in an unclear sentence: "23% of scam victims who lose money are more likely to be…" — [Feedzai blog](https://www.feedzai.com/blog/gasa-global-state-of-scams-report/)
- Search summaries of the joint report PDF place the 12-month recall window before a survey around March 2025. They say seven in ten adults "encountered" a scam, 74% of victims reported to their payment provider, and only 30% recovered some of their money. The PDF text could not be extracted, so these are search-snippet level only. — [Feedzai/GASA PDF](https://info.feedzai.com/hubfs/Feedzai_GASA-Global-State-of-Scams.pdf); [Feedzai report landing page](https://www.feedzai.com/global-state-of-scams-report-2025/)
- Secondary coverage repeats $442B and the "23% suffered financial loss" figure. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-scam-losses-442-billion/)

### Inferences
- $442B comes from survey self-reports of losses, extrapolated to the adult populations of 42 countries. The exact extrapolation method was not visible. Do not set it beside FTC/IC3 reported-loss totals as if the two measure the same thing.
- "23% of respondents lost money" is plausible. It is consistent with earlier GASA editions' "lost money" shares. But I could not tie it to primary text, so cite it as "reported by secondary coverage" or leave it out.
- Feedzai sells anti-fraud products, so this is commercially co-sponsored research.

### Gaps
- Fieldwork vendor, exact fieldwork dates, weighting and the extrapolation method were not confirmed. The full PDF was unreadable via fetch, and gasa.org's own 2025 report page was not reached.

## Claim 2: FTC (US) 2025 reported losses, older adults, and the $13.3B to $119.5B range

### Takeaway
VERIFIED and correctly attributed, with one important clarification. The $13.3B to $119.5B range is the FTC's 2025 estimate for **older adults (60+) only**. The FTC's all-ages estimate using the same method is **$38.1B to $261.5B**. All-ages reported losses for 2025 were **$15.9B** (often rounded to $16B) from about 3 million fraud reports. Older adults reported **more than $3B**. Confidence: high (medium for the all-ages range, which comes via one secondary source).

### Cited Findings
- The FTC testified to the Joint Economic Committee (Mar 2026) that consumers reported losing $15.9B to fraud in 2025. That figure rests on about 3.0M fraud reports, up from 2.6M, compared with $12.5B in 2024. Investment scams were $7.9B. Imposter scams had more than 1M reports and over $3.5B in losses. Reported losses are up about 430% since 2020. — [FTC press release, Mar 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud); [FTC testimony PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf) (figures via search summaries; ftc.gov fetch refused); [MLex](https://www.mlexwatch.com/ftcwatch/articles/2463206)
- The FTC's earlier all-ages, underreporting-adjusted estimate was up to $195.9B for 2024. — same search summaries of FTC testimony (not directly fetched)
- "Protecting Older Consumers 2025-2026: A Report of the Federal Trade Commission" is dated **19 Oct 2026**. It is the ninth annual report under the Elder Abuse Prevention and Prosecution Act, and its appendix covers FTC actions from 1 Oct 2025 to 30 Sep 2026. — [FTC page](https://www.ftc.gov/node/338049) (via search); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/); [RecordingLaw](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/ftc-older-adults-fraud-report-2026/)
- People 60+ "reported losing more than $3 billion to fraud in 2025", up from about $600M in 2020, roughly five times higher. Their investment-scam losses were about $1.059B, up 42%. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/); [FTC page](https://www.ftc.gov/node/338049)
- Caveat: no consumer reports were taken from 1 Oct to 13 Nov 2025 because of a funding lapse. The FTC imputed that gap from the 30 days on either side. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/)
- How the estimate is built: reported losses are scaled up for underreporting.
  - The high end ($119.5B) uses reporting rates from studies: 4.8% of mass-market fraud victims complained; 2.0% for losses under $1,000 and 6.7% for losses over $1,000.
  - The low end ($13.3B) assumes everyone who lost $10,000 or more reported it.
  - The FTC itself calls the estimate subject to "a considerable degree of uncertainty".
  - All-ages equivalent: $38.1B to $261.5B.
  - [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/)
- Earlier editions for comparison: older adults reported $2.4B in losses in 2024 (Dec 2025 report), and $1.9B in 2023, when the FTC estimated up to $61.5B. — [FTC Older Adults Report Dec 2025 PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf); [FTC Dec 2025 press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-issues-annual-report-congress-agencys-actions-protect-older-adults)

### Inferences
- Accurate wording: "US consumers reported $15.9B in fraud losses to the FTC in 2025; adults 60+ reported over $3B, and the FTC estimates their true losses at $13.3B–$119.5B."
- Do not use $119.5B as an all-ages figure.
- Consumer Sentinel data is self-selected reports and is not a population sample.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Oct 2026 FTC report text directly. The range and methodology details come from PPC Land's close reading of it, and the FTC node page appeared only in search results.
- The 2025 Consumer Sentinel Data Book URL itself was not retrieved.

## Claim 3: GASA State of Scams in Europe 2026 (75% encountered, 3% reported)

### Takeaway
VERIFIED on GASA's own page, published 8 Jun 2026, from a survey of 22,260 adults across Europe. Clarification: the 3% is "reported the incident to authorities" as a share of **all respondents**, not of victims. In the Germany report, the figure rises to 41% among people who lost money. Confidence: high (survey self-report).

### Cited Findings
- From GASA's summary: 22,260 adults; "75% encountered a scam in the past year"; 71% are confident they can spot scams; 8% of those exposed interacted with scammers; 6% suffered financial or data loss; "only 3% reported the incident to authorities". The full report is for GASA members only, and the page does not state the country list, fieldwork dates or methodology. — [GASA, State of Scams in Europe 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-europe-2026)
- Germany: 66% encountered a scam. Only 3% of all respondents reported to authorities, rising to 41% among those who lost money. — [GASA Germany 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-germany-2026)
- Country variation: France 74% encountered and 46% of those interacted; Spain and Portugal over 75% encountered, with 9% losing money or data. — [GASA France 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-france-2026); [GASA Spain 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-spain-2026); [GASA Portugal 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-portugal-2026)
- OCCRP's write-up uses a headline of "$57 billion toll" for Europe. I did not confirm that figure against GASA text. — [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/a-57-billion-toll-how-evolving-scams-are-fleecing-europe)

### Inferences
- The 3% figure is easy to misquote as "only 3% of victims report". The correct framing is that 3% of all adults reported. Among actual victims, the reporting rate is much higher (41% in Germany).

### Gaps
- Methodology (vendor, fieldwork dates, country list) is behind the members-only paywall. The OCCRP $57B figure is unverified.

## Claim 4: McAfee 2026 State of the Scamiverse ("one in ten Americans experienced a voice-clone scam")

### Takeaway
UNVERIFIED as stated. McAfee's Jan 2026 State of the Scamiverse report exists, but I could not find the "1 in 10 Americans experienced a voice-clone scam" figure in it. The closest "1 in 10" is from McAfee's **2023** global study of 7,054 people in 7 countries, where 1 in 10 said they had been personally targeted by an AI voice scam. The claim is likely a misattribution of that older figure. Confidence: unverified for 2026; medium that it stems from the 2023 study.

### Cited Findings
- The 2026 report (PDF dated Jan 2026) says 33% of Americans feel less confident spotting scams than a year ago, and that Americans see an average of 14 scam messages a day. The PDF timed out on fetch, so this is based on search snippets. — [McAfee State of the Scamiverse 2026 PDF](https://www.mcafee.com/blogs/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Scamiverse.pdf)
- The 2023 McAfee study, released 1 May 2023, surveyed 7,054 people in seven countries. It found a quarter had experienced or knew someone who had experienced an AI voice scam, with 1 in 10 targeted personally. — [McAfee/BusinessWire, May 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230501005587/en) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- If a voice-clone prevalence figure is needed, use the Malwarebytes 2026 survey's figure on voice-cloned calls (see Claim 6). Be aware it rests on secondary reporting.

### Gaps
- I could not read the 2026 PDF's deepfake/voice section, so the claim is neither confirmed nor ruled out for that document.

## Claim 5: Trend Micro TrendLife (Apr 2026), scam kit with voice cloning, deepfake video and fake sites for about $60/month

### Takeaway
SUBSTANTIALLY VERIFIED, with a nuance. The TrendLife blog of 16 Apr 2026 states both points, but in separate sentences. One says Scam-as-a-Service kits bundle voice cloning, deepfake video, fake website generators and messaging tools. The other says a single person can build a scam operation "often for as little as $60 per month". The blog gives no primary pricing evidence. It is a consumer-facing vendor blog, not a threat-research report. Confidence: medium.

### Cited Findings
- "AI Voice Cloning: The Scam That Sounds Exactly Like Someone You Love", TrendLife (Trend Micro), 16 Apr 2026, no author named. Scam-as-a-Service operations "package AI voice cloning, deepfake video, fake website generators, and targeted messaging tools into ready-made fraud kits". The cost to run an operation is "often for as little as $60 per month". The blog also claims 3 seconds of audio is enough to clone a voice. — [TrendLife blog](https://trendlife.com/en-us/blog/2026/04/16/ai-voice-cloning)

### Inferences
- Accurate attribution: "Trend Micro's consumer blog says…". Do not present it as measured market pricing.

### Gaps

## Claim 6: Malwarebytes "Face Value" (June 2026), 85% find scams hard to tell from the real thing; 13% have a family codeword

### Takeaway
VERIFIED with wording corrections. The report is "Face Value: How AI is reshaping trust, identity and scams", launched 10 Jun 2026. It surveyed n=1,500 adults aged 18+ in the US, UK, Austria, Germany and Switzerland in March 2026, through an unnamed independent consultant via Forsta.
- 85% is a self-reported perception: 85% "said it's hard to tell a scam apart from the real thing", up from 66% last year.
- 13% of **respondents** (not specifically families or parents) "have created a family codeword", against 81% who fear someone stealing their family's likeness.
- Confidence: high (small online survey; perception, not measured ability).

### Cited Findings
- "85% of people said it's hard to tell a scam apart from the real thing (up from 66% last year)". "81% fear someone stealing their family's likeness, yet only 13% have created a family codeword". "67% worry about voice cloning, yet only 19% have turned off voicemail recordings". Methodology: n=1,500 aged 18+ in the US, UK, AT, DE and CH; March 2026; sample split equally by gender and weighted. — [Malwarebytes press release, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/press/2026/06/10/new-malwarebytes-research-reveals-how-ai-is-reshaping-trust-scams-and-identity-theft); [Malwarebytes report page](https://www.malwarebytes.com/ai-scams)
- Secondary write-ups: half of respondents encountered an AI-driven scam in the past year, and 16% received voice-cloned calls. Gen Z exposure was 67% versus 30% for Boomers and older. Some outlets reword 85% as "can't distinguish AI content from real", which is a misquote. — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/malwarebytes-survey-finds-9-in-10-misidentify-ai-content-f759ce25); [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/security/malwarebytes-report-ai-reshaping-trust/)

### Inferences
- The headline wording "85% can't tell a scam from the real thing" overstates a perception question. Prefer "85% say it's hard to tell".

### Gaps
- The 16% voice-cloned-call figure was not confirmed on Malwarebytes' own page.

## Other primary-source headline numbers (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
National reported-loss figures for 2025: US IC3 about $20.9B; US FTC $15.9B; UK APP fraud £576.4M (total payment fraud about £1.28B); India NCRP ₹22,495 crore; Australia A$2.18B; Singapore S$913.1M. On crypto, Chainalysis puts 2025 on-chain scam revenue at ≥$14B, possibly above $17B. AI-specific: IC3 counted more than 22,000 complaints referencing AI, with adjusted losses above $893M. For deepfakes, Deloitte projects US gen-AI-enabled fraud losses reaching $40B by 2027 (US, not global). Arup lost about US$25M in a Hong Kong deepfake case (Jan 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report** (released Apr 2026). Figures via secondary coverage; the ic3.gov PDF was not opened.
  - 1,008,597 complaints; about $20.877B in losses, up about 26%.
  - Investment fraud $8.6B; BEC $3B; tech support $2.1B.
  - Cyber-enabled fraud about $17.6–17.7B (sources differ).
  - Crypto more than $11.36B.
  - People 60+: 201,266 complaints and about $7.7B.
  - More than 22,000 complaints referencing AI, with "adjusted losses" above $893M.
  - [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/fbi-internet-crime-complaint-center-annual-cybercrime-report/); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/cybercrime-trends-to-watch-takeaways-from-the-fbis-2025-ic3-annual-report/); [The Record](https://therecord.media/cyber-fraud-surges-to-17-billion-fbi-ic3)
  - Confidence: medium-high.
- **FTC vs IC3 conflict:** the two are different complaint systems with overlapping but distinct populations. IC3 covers cybercrime including BEC and businesses; the FTC covers consumers. Do not add them together. — inference from the above
- **UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2026** (Jun 2026, covering 2025). Figures via secondary coverage; the PDF was not opened.
  - APP fraud losses £576.4M, up 19%; 248,070 APP cases, up 7%.
  - APP investment scams £221.5M, up 40%.
  - Unauthorised fraud £703.4M, down 5%.
  - Total about £1.28B; £1.68B prevented.
  - 66% of APP cases started online and 17% via telecoms.
  - [UK Finance Fraud Report 2026 PDF](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/system/files/2026-06/UK%20Finance%20Fraud%20Report%202026.pdf); [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/financial-crime-fraud-prevention/uk-payment-fraud-losses-topped-1-28bn-in-2025-new-uk-finance-report-finds); [Payment Expert](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/06/15/uk-finance-fraud-report-2026/)
  - Confidence: medium-high.
- **India (I4C / NCRP / CFCFRMS), 2025**
  - Reported financial-fraud losses ₹22,495 crore; 24,02,579 financial-fraud complaints in 2025 (19,18,835 in 2024).
  - Earlier years: ₹22,848 crore in 2024 and ₹7,465 crore in 2023.
  - CFCFRMS saved more than ₹8,189 crore across more than 23.61 lakh complaints.
  - These figures come from a parliamentary reply (Feb 2026) as reported by Indian media. I did not locate the pib.gov.in / mha.gov.in primary.
  - [Dynamite News](https://www.dynamitenews.com/national/cyber-frauds-mount-to-rs22495-crore-in-2025-over-rs8000-crore-saved-through-rapid-response-system); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/over-24-lakh-cyber-complaints-22495-crore-fraud-reported-in-2025)
  - **Conflict on "28 lakh":** that figure (28.15 lakh) is *total cybercrime complaints*, not financial-fraud complaints (about 24 lakh). — [TechStory](https://techstory.in/india-loses-%E2%82%B922495-crore-to-cyber-crimes-in-2025-as-cases-surge-past-28-lakh/)
  - Moneylife reports an I4C projection that true 2025 losses could reach about ₹1.2 lakh crore. This is a projection and is unverified at the primary level. — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/housing-society-problems-and-solutions-occupancy-rights-leakage-disputes-and-agm-decisions/article/fraud-alert-india-is-losing-over-22000-crore-a-year-in-cyber-scams-and-the-worst-is-yet-to-come/80612.html)
  - Confidence: medium.

  - 481,523 reports, of which 274,577 involved losses totalling A$2.18B.
  - Up 7.8% on 2024 and 29.7% below the 2022 peak of A$3.1B.
  - Investment scams A$837.7M.
  - People aged 65+ accounted for 26.5% of Scamwatch losses.
  - [NASC report PDF](https://www.nasc.gov.au/system/files/targeting-scams-report-2025.pdf); [NASC/ACCC media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/continued-action-critical-to-combat-fraud-as-annual-scam-losses-exceed-2-billion); [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/research-and-resources/targeting-scams-report)

- **Singapore SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025** (Feb 2026).
  - Losses about S$913.1M, down 17.9% from S$1,112.4M.
  - Cases 41,974, down 24.8%.
  - About S$140.5M recovered; median loss S$1,644.
  - [SPF brief PDF](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Advisories/Scams/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf); [SPF Police Life](https://www.police.gov.sg/Media-Hub/Police-Life/2026/02/Scams-and-Cybercrime-Fell-by-Almost-a-Quarter-in-2025)
  - Confidence: high.
- **Chainalysis 2026 Crypto Crime Report** (scams chapter, about 13 Jan 2026). Via secondary coverage.
  - At least $14B in on-chain scam revenue in 2025, possibly above $17B as more addresses are identified; 2024 was revised to $12B from an initial $9.9B.
  - Impersonation scams grew about 1,400%.
  - AI-linked operations averaged $3.2M versus $719K for those without AI links.
  - Total illicit volume at least $154B, driven by sanctioned entities.
  - [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/crypto-scammers-stole-a-record-14bn-in-2025/); [GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/chainalysis-crypto-crime-2026-record-illicit-volume/)
  - Confidence: medium. These are on-chain estimates that are revised upward over time.
- **Deloitte Center for Financial Services:** gen-AI could push US fraud losses from $12.3B (2023) to $40B by 2027, a 32% CAGR. This is a projection and is US only. — [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/deepfake-scam-businesses-finance-threat/726043/) (Deloitte primary not fetched); confidence medium.
- **Arup, Hong Kong (Jan 2024):** an employee joined a deepfake video call impersonating the CFO and made 15 transfers totalling about HK$200M / US$25.5M to five accounts. — [WEF](https://weforum.org/stories/2025/07/why-detecting-dangerous-ai-is-key-to-keeping-trust-alive); [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/deepfake-scam-businesses-finance-threat/726043/); confidence high (widely confirmed, and Arup acknowledged it).

### Inferences
- Different measurement types:
  - **Reported losses:** FTC, IC3, UK Finance, NCRP, NASC and SPF.
  - **Modelled or estimated:** FTC's underreporting ranges; Chainalysis on-chain estimates.
  - **Survey-extrapolated:** GASA's $442B.
  - **Projections:** Deloitte $40B; the I4C ₹1.2 lakh crore figure.
- The gap between FTC reported ($15.9B) and estimated (up to $261.5B all ages) shows the 10x+ underreporting multiplier. That helps reconcile GASA's much larger global figure.
- UK Finance figures cover only bank-reported payment fraud. That partly explains the lower loss levels.

### Gaps
- No direct reads of ic3.gov, ukfinance.org.uk, or pib.gov.in PDFs. Figures rely on consistent secondary coverage.
- I found no credible *global* deepfake-fraud loss total. Vendor statistics such as Deepstrike are aggregator-grade and were excluded.
